# Multi-Objective Candidate Scoring (PY-018)

The `app.optimisation.scoring` module provides deterministic, multi-objective ranking of AC electrically evaluated PNC candidates. It determines which PNC scenario generated by PY-017 is mathematically the "best" under a configurable policy and provides explainable reasons for its selection.

## Relationship to Preliminary Route Scoring

The existing spatial scorer in `app.algorithms.route_scoring` evaluates constructability constraints (environmental overlaps, parcel counts, road crossings).
PY-018 introduces a completely separate module (`app.optimisation.scoring`) focused purely on **physical routed length and AC electrical performance**.

**Crucial distinctions:**
- The preliminary spatial scorer uses a **"lower is better" penalty convention**.
- The PY-018 multi-objective scorer uses a **"higher is better" benefit convention**, where the best possible score for a metric is 1.0 and the worst is 0.0.
- They are currently not composed. The PY-018 scorer only sees route length, active losses, cable loading, and voltage margin.

## Eligibility and Comparison Cohorts

Before scoring occurs, candidates are filtered for hard feasibility:
1. **Convergence**: Load flow must have converged.
2. **Electrical Validity**: No overloads or voltage limit violations.
3. **Completeness**: All required metrics must be present and finite.

Ineligible candidates are disqualified and receive a `total_benefit_score` of `None`. However, they remain in the output to provide visibility into *why* they failed.

Candidates are also strictly checked for context mismatch. They must all share the same `comparison_group_id` and `electrical_context_id`. Comparing candidates evaluated under different operating points or with different cable libraries is structurally prohibited.

## Metrics and Direction

The following metrics are extracted from `PNCScenario` and `LoadFlowNetworkResult`:

- **Route Length (m)**: Physical routed network length. Lower is better.
- **Active Loss (MW)**: Total active power loss. Lower is better.
- **Cable Loading (%)**: Maximum segment loading. Lower is better.
- **Voltage Margin (pu)**: The minimum margin to either the upper or lower voltage limit. Specifically, `min(min_v - min_limit, max_limit - max_v)`. **Higher is better**.

## Normalization Equations

Metrics are evaluated across the *eligible* cohort. The min and max values establish the normalization bounds.

For "lower is better" metrics (length, loss, loading):
`normalized_benefit = (max - val) / (max - min)`

For "higher is better" metrics (voltage margin):
`normalized_benefit = (val - min) / (max - min)`

### Constant Range Behavior

If all eligible candidates have the identical value for a metric (i.e. `max == min`), the metric is considered "constant" and provides no discriminatory power. In this case, `normalized_benefit = 0.0` for all candidates. The weight of the constant metric is **not** automatically redistributed, as doing so would silently alter the configured decision policy. 
As a consequence, if there is only a single eligible candidate, its total benefit score will be `0.0` (but it will still be recommended).

### Limitations of Relative Min-Max Normalization

Relative min-max normalization means scores are entirely dependent on the cohort. A candidate might score 1.0 on losses because it is the best *in this specific set*, even if its absolute losses are poor. Absolute thresholds are currently handled entirely by the hard eligibility checks (e.g., maximum loading limit), leaving soft scoring to resolve relative differences.

## Scoring and Tie-Breaking

Normalized benefits are multiplied by their configured weights and summed (`math.fsum()`) to produce the `total_benefit_score`. No premature rounding is performed.

Eligible candidates are ranked by:
1. `total_benefit_score` (Descending)
2. `total_route_length_m` (Ascending)
3. `total_active_loss_mw` (Ascending)
4. `scenario_id` (Ascending)

This ensures a perfectly deterministic order even when total scores are identical.

## Explainability and Baseline Selection

The recommended candidate is accompanied by structured `RecommendationReason`s explaining *why* it won (e.g. `LOWEST_CABLE_LOADING`, `HIGHEST_TOTAL_BENEFIT`). 
A reason like "Has the lowest maximum cable loading" is only emitted if the winner actually has the absolute lowest raw value in the cohort for that metric, not just because the metric is heavily weighted.

The winner is also compared against the baseline scenario to produce absolute and relative improvement metrics (e.g. "-5.4% route length"). The baseline is identified uniquely by checking `scenario.parameters.strategy == ScenarioStrategy.BASELINE`, ensuring resilient discovery even if it is not labeled `SCN-001`.
